Research protocol / current prototype

Methodology & sources

This page describes what the current implementation actually does. It is intentionally separate from the broader long-term ambition of SOCIETY-X.

Current data coverage

The current quantitative pipeline combines a predefined set of World Bank World Development Indicators (WDI) with selected Our World in Data (OWID) country-year charts for 2000–2023. GBIF contributes a separate annual count of coordinate-bearing occurrence records; this is not used as a direct measure of biodiversity. OpenAlex supplies bibliographic leads, not empirical validation. NASA POWER and PubMed remain catalogue entries pending dedicated connectors.

Indicator codes, retrieval URLs and timestamps are recorded with each run. See the research log for a readable summary of the latest cycle.

Analysis procedure

For each pair of quantitative datasets, Pearson's correlation coefficient is calculated separately within each year across countries with observations for both variables. A year requires at least 40 countries, and an indicator pair requires at least eight usable years. Candidate pairs are ranked by mean absolute yearly correlation and direction consistency. For up to 15 leading candidates, a skeptical sensitivity layer also calculates Spearman rank correlations on the same yearly samples and reports direction agreement and estimator gaps.

What this does not establish

The analysis does not establish causation, novelty, statistical significance, predictive usefulness or a mechanism. It does not correct for all confounders, selection effects, measurement differences or multiple comparisons.

Initial confounder sensitivity check

For leading candidate pairs that do not themselves include GDP per capita, the prototype also computes a within-year partial Pearson correlation after linear adjustment for GDP per capita, using only countries with all three observations. This is a single-control diagnostic, not a complete confounder model. GDP per capita may be a confounder, mediator, proxy or collider depending on the research question; therefore adjusted and unadjusted results must both be interpreted cautiously. It does not establish causal effects.

How to interpret candidate signals

  • Correlation is descriptive. It shows that two measured variables move together in the sampled data.
  • Country-level patterns are not individual-level effects. Aggregated associations can conceal substantial within-country variation.
  • Missing data matters. Different country samples can change the interpretation of a comparison.
  • Novelty requires a literature review. A pattern found by the prototype is not automatically new to science.
  • Estimator agreement is limited evidence. Pearson and Spearman agreement reduces concern that a result is solely driven by linear-scale assumptions, but it does not rule out confounding or selection bias.
  • Replication matters. Stronger claims require independent sources, robustness checks and suitable follow-up designs.

Known implementation limitations

The current engine performs a bounded statistical scan of a small, predefined indicator set. It autonomously selects follow-up questions using a deterministic planner, but does not yet autonomously survey the wider internet, discover arbitrary new variables, build a persistent knowledge graph, generate and compare causal models, or validate research methods. The Pearson–Spearman comparison is a preliminary sensitivity diagnostic, not causal falsification or independent replication. It is a scheduled prototype, not a continuously running autonomous agent.

Research development direction

The intended next stages are broader source discovery, source and licence metadata, a persistent knowledge layer, autonomous research planning, variable construction, falsification and replication tools, and measured self-improvement in isolated environments. Claims of capability will be updated only when the corresponding functionality is implemented and tested.

SOCIETY-X · Methodology is versioned as the implementation changes. Current limitations are part of the public record.
